# Security

What security is about is getting access to things that you should not have access to.

The easiest thing is help explain this is passwords. We all have passwords and know we don't want other people to
have ours! When making software, it is super important to handle sensistive data, like passwords, and restricted areas, like
access to a database, with the utmost of care.

In our world, one of the most common ways to gain access to a system is not through clever coding/hacking/technology skills
(even though, those obviously work too), but actually in a much dumber way: just straight up asking for it/ already having it.

Think phishing email, scammers trying to get your data, and disgruntled employees. They are not really "hacking" in the
traditional sense of "breaking the mainframe" or whatever, they are just socially trying to manipulate people into giving them info.
In the case of the disgruntled employee, they often would ALREADY have access to info that could cause harm.

Preventing these would often involve informing people to not get hacked. 

In the case of the disgruntled employee, maybe think about who you give access to what in your company.
